Where to stay in Ascoli Piceno

Inside the walls, just outside or up on the hill: what actually changes, from people who live here.

The real question

Ascoli is small, but the area matters

You can walk all of Ascoli Piceno: from the walls to the Roman Bridge is a good twenty-minute stroll, and the historic centre is a compact block of travertine you cross in a quarter of an hour. Which is why the question is not how close you are, but how you arrive and where you leave the car.

The old town is largely a restricted traffic zone. Anyone arriving by car, by motorbike or with bikes on the roof ends up solving the parking question before they have even unpacked. Here are the four areas where people stay in Ascoli, and what each one really gives you.

The four areas of Ascoli

None of them is wrong: it depends how you travel.

Area 1

Inside the walls, in the old town

Piazza del Popolo, Piazza Arringo, the Rue: you step out of the door and you are already in the scene. Perfect if you arrive by train or by bus and want the squares late into the evening.

Worth knowing: the restricted traffic zone, parking to be solved separately, and lively evenings right under the windows.

Area 2

Just outside the walls

Porta Maggiore, Campo Parignano, viale De Gasperi: five to ten minutes on foot from the centre and parking is easier. The practical choice if you are staying a single night.

Worth knowing: you are on the city's through roads, so more traffic and less of a view.

Area 3

The hill, beyond the Roman Bridge

You cross the Roman Bridge, climb towards the Solestà quarter and in ten to fifteen minutes on foot you are out of the noise, with the historic centre laid out below you. You park freely along the street, outside the restricted zone: the car stays put for your whole stay.

This is the area for people arriving by bike, by motorbike or on foot along the walking routes, and for anyone who wants the city within strolling distance and silence when they come back.

Area 4

The countryside and the Piceno villages

Farmhouses among the hills, Offida, Ripatransone, the vineyards of the Piceno. Complete silence and often lower prices: the right choice if your trip is about villages and wineries.

Worth knowing: you need the car for every move, dinner in town included.

The traffic zone

Where do you park in Ascoli Piceno?

The old town is a restricted traffic zone. The easy parking is just outside the walls and in the hillside areas, where you park freely along the street. Staying outside the zone is the simplest way to leave the car still and see the city on foot.

Distances

How far is the hill from the centre?

Ten to fifteen minutes on foot: down to Solestà, across the Roman Bridge, and you are a couple of minutes from Piazza del Popolo.

The Quintana

Where should I stay during the Quintana?

During the Giostra, in July and August, the centre is full and closed to traffic: staying just outside the walls lets you reach the procession on foot and get back easily. These are the busiest two weeks of the year, so move early.

Before you book

Three things to know about Ascoli

The travertine changes colour. The squares of Ascoli are paved in pale stone: at sunset they turn golden. From the hill you watch it happen across the whole city.

Two nights is the right measure. One day for Ascoli — the squares, the Rue, Forte Malatesta, the fried stuffed olives — and one for the villages of the Piceno, Offida first among them.

July and August are Quintana months. If your trip falls there, book well ahead: the town fills up and availability closes fast.
